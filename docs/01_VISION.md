## The north star

A consumer should know where their food comes from, who grew it, and how to
be fed by a place rather than fed by a system. The product of that knowing
is not a one-time revelation — it is a daily relationship.

AgriShala exists to make that relationship **practical**: weekly food from a
specific piece of land, weekends on that land, and a farm that can sustain
itself while it sustains the people it feeds.

We are not building a marketplace. We are not building a content site. We
are building the smallest possible bridge between a city plate and the soil
that filled it, in a form that a working person can actually live with.

## The three pillars

The whole concept rests on three pillars. Every feature, every page, every
zoning decision should be defensible against at least one of these.

### 1. Subscription — *know the field that feeds you*

**Intent.** A consumer subscribes to a defined piece of land inside a 1-acre
(or larger) farm. They receive **periodic photo updates** from their piece
of the farm — sowing, growing, harvest, packing — and then a **weekly door
delivery** on a specified day. They eat from a known field, not from an
abstract supply chain.

**Who it serves.**

- The subscriber: a household that wants accountability in its food
  without becoming a farmer.
- The city dweller who has lost the language of seasons, soil, and effort.

**What it produces.**

- A predictable, traceable weekly basket.
- An ongoing visual journal of the plot (sow → grow → harvest → pack).
- Trust earned over weeks, not at a single sales touch.

**What it costs.** Subscription fee + per-basket logistics + the agronomy
labour to run the zone. See `04_BUSINESS_MODEL.md`.

### 2. Experience — *live what you eat*

**Intent.** The same farm hosts the subscriber and the curious. It offers
**stay**, **local excursion** (BRT wildlife, Soliga-adjacent culture,
Tibetan settlement, Jenumutti temple), **cooking** (field-to-kitchen with
the farm's own produce), and **events** (workshops, ceremonies, retreats,
celebrations). The farm becomes a place you *visit*, not just a warehouse
you receive parcels from.

**Who it serves.**

- Existing workshop participants (continuity of the current scroll narrative).
- Subscribers who want to see their plot in person.
- Urban groups looking for offsite venues rooted in something real.
- Domestic and intentional-travel tourists.

**What it produces.**

- A second revenue line that does not depend on agronomy throughput.
- Cultural and ecological place-making (the BRT reserve, the Soliga
  community, the Jenumutti temple, the Tibetan settlement are all
  within reach).
- A reason for subscribers to deepen their commitment.

**What it costs.** Hospitality infrastructure (stay units, kitchen upgrade),
staffing, insurance, event ops. See `04_BUSINESS_MODEL.md`.

### 3. Sustenance — *make the farm self-standing*

**Intent.** The farm must outlast the founder, the season, the grant. It
achieves this through **vertical integration**: finished products (ghee,
honey, soap, pickles, millet flours, value-added snacks) sold to a wider
market, and through **regenerative practices** — **Gau Shala** (cattle
integration for manure, urine, biogas, soil biology), **Agnihotra**
(timed fire offering as part of the integrated pest and atmospheric
management), composting, seed-saving, mixed cropping, rainwater
harvesting — that close nutrient, water, and energy loops on the farm.

**Who it serves.**

- The farm itself, first.
- The wider market that wants honest, identity-preserved products.
- Future AgriShala replication sites (the seed farm is the recipe).

**What it produces.**

- A farm that decreases its dependence on external inputs every year.
- A retail line (D2C and select stockists).
- A replicable model. See `03_SEED_FARM.md` and `12_ROADMAP.md`.

**What it costs.** Capital for cattle, kitchen, packing shed, storage.
Operational discipline more than anything else. See `10_FARM_OPERATIONS.md`.

## The "seed farm" idea

The current site is a 2-day workshop at **Lokkanahalli**, on the edge of the
**Biligiri Rangaswamy Temple (BRT) Tiger Reserve**, run by Jayapal (founder
of Bamboo Cafe). See `03_SEED_FARM.md` for the full map.

That location is the **seed farm** — the first site that proves the model
before we replicate it. The three pillars are not three separate
businesses; they are three layers on the same piece of land:

```
                       ┌─────────────────────────────┐
                       │      vertical-integration   │
                       │      retail & processing    │  ← pillar 3
                       │  (ghee, honey, soap, etc.)  │
                       └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                                      │
                       ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
                       │      hospitality & events   │  ← pillar 2
                       │  (stay, cooking, excursions)│
                       └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                                      │
                       ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
                       │    weekly subscription +    │  ← pillar 1
                       │    photo updates + delivery │
                       └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                                      │
                                  the land
```

## What we will *not* do

In the spirit of `02_CONCEPT_BRIEF.md`, the platform is not:

- A generic organic-food marketplace.
- A farming-as-a-service SaaS.
- A wellness tourism app.

It is one farm, scaled, that you can either eat from, stay at, or learn
from. Its defensibility is its specificity.

---

## The test of any decision

Before we ship a feature, a zone, a page, or a paragraph, we ask:

1. Does it shorten the distance between a plate and a field?
2. Does it make the farm more self-sustaining, not less?
3. Does it preserve the voice — *poetic, hand-made, field-journal* — that
   already lives in `components/sections/`?

If the answer to two out of three is no, we don't ship it.
