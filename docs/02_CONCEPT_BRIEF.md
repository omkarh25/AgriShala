## The problem

A city dweller does not know where his food comes from, and has no idea
about the people who work hard to make it available to every person. The
food on his plate is correct in nutrition and wrong in relationship. He
eats from a system he cannot name.

## The idea

**AgriShala** is a working farm and a small, slow product built around it.
We turn the distance between a city plate and a field of soil into
something a working household can cross every week, and eventually every
quarter or weekend.

The platform is structured as three pillars on one farm:

| Pillar       | What it is                                                          | What the consumer does                              |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Subscription | The 1-acre is zoned. You subscribe to a plot. Weekly delivery to your door, with photo updates from your plot beforehand. | Eats from a known field. |
| Experience   | The farm has stay, a kitchen, and proximity to BRT wildlife, the Soliga community, the Jenumutti temple, the Tibetan settlement. | Visits. Cooks. Celebrates. |
| Sustenance   | Gau Shala, Agnihotra, composting, seed-saving, value-added products (ghee, honey, soap, pickles) for the wider market. | Trusts the farm to outlast the trend. |

## The lighthouse

The first site is **Lokkanahalli**, on the edge of the **BRT Tiger Reserve**
in Karnataka, run by **Jayapal** (Bamboo Cafe). It already hosts a 2-day
immersive workshop — the scroll-narrative site in this repo — that has
proven the educational angle. We expand it outward, not away from it.

## The ask (of this concept-brief round)

A shared, written alignment on:

1. What the three pillars promise and what they do not.
2. What the seed farm is and is not.
3. What the next 12 months look like.
