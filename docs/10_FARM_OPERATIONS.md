# 10 — Farm Operations

> The *Sustenance* pillar in operational depth. This document is what an
> agronomist or junior farmer can use on the ground. Read alongside
> `11_FARM_ZONING.md` for the spatial map, and `13_GLOSSARY.md` for any
> term you don't recognise.

> **Living document.** Each section is meant to be edited as the playbook
> learns from the field. Ops owns the source of truth.

---

## 0. Principles

1. **Polyculture over monoculture.** A handful of crops per zone, in
   rotation. No zone is a single crop forever.
2. **Closed loops.** What the farm produces should feed the farm:
   cow-dung feeds compost feeds soil; urine feeds preparation feeds
   pest management; crop residue feeds the cattle. We buy from outside
   what we cannot produce internally (rock phosphate, bio-pesticides
   for emergencies only).
3. **Native breeds over imported.** Local cattle (Malnad Gidda /
   Hallikar where available) and indigenous seed stock. Cross-bred or
   exotic stock only for clear agronomic reasons.
4. **Practice over product.** The Homa matters because of when and why
   it's done, not because it's data. We log it for our records, not
   to chase a metric.
5. **Document one thing a day.** A junior farmer writes one line in a
   notebook — a weather note, a pest sighting, a cow's mood. The
   platform absorbs selected notes later (Phase 3+).

## 1. Gau Shala (the cattle integration)

### Why

The cow is the centre of the farm's nutrient cycle. She gives:

- **Dung** → dung cakes (fuel + Homa offering), gobar slurry (biogas +
  liquid manure), compost feedstock.
- **Urine** → pest-deterrent sprays (with neem), Agnihotra vessel
  liquid, soil activator.
- **Milk** → household consumption + value-added (ghee, soap).
- **Bull-power** (when present, with bullocks) → ploughing, carting,
  sugarcane-crushing, oil-pressing. Eliminates diesel for many tasks.
- **Soil biology.** Standing livestock in a rotational paddock is one
  of the cheapest fertility inputs we have.

### Herd size — for a 1-acre seed farm

```
Recommended starting herd (Phase 3): 4 cows + 1 bull / calf

Why 5 animals on 1 acre of *zone* land:
- A cow needs roughly 15–25 kg of green fodder per day.
- 4 cows × 20 kg × 365 days ≈ 29 tonnes of green fodder / yr.
- That fits in Z4 (Gau Shala pasture / fodder zone, see
  `11_FARM_ZONING.md`) of roughly 405 m² — but only if Z4 is heavily
  intercropped with hybrid napier, sesbania, and seasonal grasses.
- The 5th animal provides non-cyclical stall periods, drying-off, and
  bull-power without forcing the herd to be productive at all times.
```

Herd growth is a Phase 4 decision (the seed farm might cap at ~8 head
on this acreage; further growth requires more land).

### Daily routine (proposed)

```
05:30   Homa (see §2)
05:45   Cows released to day paddock; water replenished.
06:00   Milking (if lactating); cow-dung collected, water troughs
        cleaned.
07:00   Dung cakes shaped (round, sun-dried on a sloped pad with
        roof). Dung slurry prepared for compost.
09:00   Cows tethered in sun for part of the morning (winter) or
        kept in shade (summer). Water offered twice more.
12:00   Mid-day feed of dry fodder.
15:00   Afternoon water.
17:00   Cows returned to paddock for night grazing; closed in only in
        extreme weather or predator pressure from the BRT.
18:00   Evening milking. Second dung collection. Cow-urine collected
        into jerry-cans (used within 24h for sprays; remainder in
        sealed drums, not plastic).
```

### Fodder plan

- **Perennial.** Hybrid napier on bund lines; sesbania for protein;
  subabul (Leucaena) on a hedge.
- **Kharif.** Maize + cowpea intercrop; sorghum.
- **Rabi.** Lucerne + oats.
- **Dry fodder reserve.** Always 3–4 months of paddy straw / ragi straw
  baled and stored under roof.

### Health

We are not a vet clinic. The health rules:

- A registered local vet visits **monthly** and on-call.
- Vaccinations follow the state schedule (FMD, HS, BQ).
- Tick management is **manual** with a daily grooming pass; we do **not**
  use synthetic acaricides on milking cows.
- An animal in distress is separated, not tied.
- Calving happens in a clean, straw-bedded pen with the herd in
  earshot; no human intervention unless the cow cannot deliver in
  ~4 hours.

### What we explicitly do not do

- **No oxytocin injections** to "let down" milk. A calf or the natural
  let-down reflex does this.
- **No synthetic growth hormone.**
- **No early-weaning for milk yield.** Calves get the first milk for at
  least 3 months.

### Outputs and where they go

| Output         | Goes to                                                       |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dung cakes     | Homa fire + household cooking fuel + emergency stove fuel.    |
| Gobar slurry   | Biogas plant → cooking gas + liquid effluent → compost.       |
| Cow urine      | Neem-garlic-cow-urine spray (Pest Mgmt), Homa vessel.        |
| Milk (excess)  | Ghee + paneer for café + subscriber add-ons (Phase 3).        |
| Calves         | Herd replenishment; one or two sold/year to a local dairy.    |

## 2. Agnihotra / Homa

### What it is

Agnihotra is a Vedic fire offering performed at sunrise and sunset, in
a copper pyramid, with a measured offering of cow-dung cakes, ghee,
rice, and specific dried twigs (**samidhas**). We treat it as one of
several **integrated atmospheric and pest-management practices** —
not as a panacea, not as a marketing ritual.

We commit to it because:

1. The historical record from Homa farms in Maharashtra, Karnataka,
   and elsewhere reports measurably lower pest pressure and improved
   soil biology within 1–2 seasons of consistent practice.
2. It disciplines the day. Two fixed points (sunrise, sunset) bookend
   the agronomy team's work.
3. It feeds the soil and the cattle (ash from the offerings is a
   micronutrient source and an ingredient in foliar sprays).

### What we promise

- Two daily offerings at the seed farm: at sunrise and at sunset,
   defined by **local solar time** (not clock time). We do not skip.
- The Homa is open to visitors — it is a small ritual that anchors
   the day, not a performance.
- The ash is collected and used: mixed with cow urine for foliar
   sprays; dusted on seed beds; given to the cattle in feed.

### What we do not promise

- We do not promise absence of pests. We promise they are lower.
- We do not promise higher yields than input-driven monoculture. We
  promise *different* yields — healthier soil, polyculture buffers,
  fewer input costs.
- We do not turn it into a brand asset on the homepage. The `/journal`
  section can show the day's Homa, but the homepage stays a story
  about food, not metaphysics.

### Records

We log one Homa per session into `HomaRecord` (see
`07_DATA_MODEL.md`, Phase 3). The point is **continuity**, not data:

- who performed it,
- the local solar conditions,
- the offering sizes,
- a one-line intention (optional).

If we ever miss a session (extreme weather, no trained staff), we
document the gap. Continuity is what makes the practice effective;
consistency of record is how we keep ourselves honest.

### Materials

| Material          | Source                                              |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Copper pyramid    | Standard size from a known Homa supply house.       |
| Cow-dung cakes    | From our own cows (`§1`).                          |
| Ghee              | From our own cows (small quantity, kept on hand).  |
| Rice              | Locally purchased, organic.                        |
| Samidhas          | A known list, sourced once a year.                 |

### Training

Two team members must be trained and able to perform Agnihotra at any
given time. We invest in training, not in ritual secrecy.

## 3. Composting & soil biology

### Why we don't buy fertilizer

The seed farm will not purchase synthetic N-P-K. The principle is the
same as for Homa: we're not ideologically opposed to inputs; we are
structurally building a system that doesn't need them. That's the
sustenance.

### Composting recipe (sheet compost + windrow)

```
Inputs:
  - Green: kitchen waste from Bamboo Cafe, fresh crop residue, weeds
           (pre-seed), cow-dung (1:10 by volume).
  - Brown: paddy straw, dried leaves, sawdust.
  - Catalyst: cow-urine slurry + a handful of mature compost / soil.

Windrow: ~ 1.5 m wide × 0.9 m tall, length as needed. Turned weekly.
Maturity: 60–90 days in summer, 90–120 in monsoon.

C:N ratio target: 25:1 to 30:1 (a handful test — if it smells of
ammonia, more brown; if it doesn't heat up, more green).

Place: a sloped, roofed, brick-bottomed yard near the cows. Leachate
drains to a sump and is reused for composting.
```

### Vermicompost

A second pile, fed with pre-composted material, hosts
*Eudrilus eugeniae* or *Perionyx excavatus*. Useful for kitchen-waste
side streams and seedling nurseries.

### Bio-fertilizers (small-batch, recurring)

- **Jeevamrutha.** Cow-dung + cow-urine + jaggery + pulse flour +
  soil. Fermented 4–7 days. Applied as a soil drench at fortnightly
  intervals in the growing season.
- **Panchagavya.** Cow-dung + cow-urine + milk + curd + ghee + jaggery
  + banana + tender coconut water. Foliar spray at 3% solution.
- **Beejamrutha.** Seed-coating solution before sowing: cow-dung +
  cow-urine + lime + water.

The three together are cheap, recurring, and replace most "plant
tonic" purchases we might otherwise consider.

### Soil testing

- **Annually, before kharif sowing**, send a composite sample per zone
  to the nearest KVK or private lab.
- Track N-P-K, organic carbon, pH, electrical conductivity, and any
  micro-nutrient trends over years.

### Mulching

Every inter-row in every zone gets a thick (5–10 cm) mulch of
**dried leaf + straw** during the dry months. In the wet months,
mulch is thinner (3–4 cm) to avoid waterlogging.

### Composting on the platform

- Phase 1 has no soil-feature on the website.
- Phase 3 may publish a *yearly soil report* per zone — written, not
  viz-heavy — as part of the brand promise.

## 4. Seed-saving

A seed is not just a yield. It's the next season.

### What we save

- **Self-pollinated crops** first: ragi, horsegram, toor, cowpea,
  greens (*amaranth*, *fenugreek*), sesame.
- **Cross-pollinated crops** require isolation distance: we grow them
  in larger blocks in Z4 (Gau Shala fodder) where seed purity matters
  less.

### Process

```
Select → Tag → Dry → Thresh → Winnow → Grade → Store → Test
```

- Tag the best 5% of plants in a subplot *before* harvest.
- Dry on cloth, not on the bare ground.
- Store in **clean, dry, airtight** tins or glass jars, labelled with
  crop, lot number, year.
- Germination test before each kharif; discard any lot below 75%.

### What we buy

Only what we cannot save: hybrid napier (perennial, sterile),
foundation seed from a known source for cross-pollinated crops like
okra and brinjal, and *some* vegetable hybrids for kitchen use.

## 5. Pest & disease management

Sequence, every time:

1. **Cultural.** Spacing, timing, intercropping, trap crops.
2. **Mechanical.** Hand-picking, sticky traps, pheromone traps, bird
   perches, netting.
3. **Botanical.** Neem oil (5 ml/L), neem-garlic-chilli emulsion,
   cow-urine + neem foliar.
4. **Microbial.** *Trichoderma viride*, *Pseudomonas fluorescens*,
   *Beauveria bassiana*, *Metarhizium*, *Bacillus thuringiensis* —
   applied preventatively, not curatively.
5. **Mineral.** Bordeaux mixture (1%) only when fungal pressure is
   measurable. Sulphur dust in extremis.

We never use neonicotinoids, organophosphates, or synthetic
pyrethroids on the seed farm. **There are no exceptions.**

## 6. Water

### Sources (likely on the seed farm)

- One open well or bore well (existing).
- Roof catchment from all buildings.
- A small recharge trench along the boundary.

### Use

- Drip for Z3 (Millets & Pulses) and Z2 (Greens).
- Flood-bed for paddy block, if any (Phase 3+).
- Sprinkler for Z4 (fodder).
- Mulch + interculture everywhere possible to reduce direct evaporation.

### Discipline

- Watering only at dawn or dusk in summer.
- A weekly crop-water-budget per zone, written down.
- A target ET-reduction of 25% over two years (mulch + drip + better
  species mix).

## 7. Labour, cadence, rhythm

### Staffing (1 acre)

Minimum paid roles for a Phase-3 seed farm:

| Role                                  | Count | Notes                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Farm lead / agronomist                | 1     | Full-time.                                       |
| Gau Shala caretaker                   | 1     | Full-time; handles 5 animals + daily Homa.       |
| Pack-house & delivery lead            | 1     | Full-time; baskets + one weekly delivery run.    |
| Café (Bamboo Cafe, existing)          | as is | Independent of the platform; overlapping labour. |
| Seasonal labour                       | 3–5   | Pulled from Lokkanahalli + a Soliga rota.        |

We commit to **at least one team member from the surrounding villages**
on payroll, including from the Soliga community. The platform does not
parachute in.

### Cadence

- **Daily.** Homa × 2; cattle routine; pack-house tasks; one
  photo-update draft.
- **Weekly.** Subscribers' photo digest (Tue/Wed); weekly delivery;
  agronomy walk-around and notes.
- **Monthly.** Soil + pest scouting; compost turning audit.
- **Seasonal.** Crop plan resets; rotation planning; community
  gathering.

## 8. Do's and don'ts for new team members

**Do.**
- Walk every zone at least once a day.
- Hand-write one observation per day, even if it's small.
- Ask the elders before trying a new practice.

**Don't.**
- Apply anything to the soil that was not produced on or near the farm.
- Promise a subscriber an item that isn't in the soil test result.
- Skip Homa.
- Buy seeds of questionable origin because they're cheap.
- Use a weed-whacker when a hand-hoe will do.
